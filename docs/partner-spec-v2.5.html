SECTION 1 — SYSTEM OBJECTIVES & DATA ARCHITECTURE

Slayer Terminal: Product Specification & Technical Architecture

Section Context & Purpose

This section outlines the primary mission of Slayer Terminal as a high-precision, non-executing intelligence and trade-signal generator. It details the underlying high-frequency data ingestion pipeline, point-in-time time-series storage using TimescaleDB hypertables, nanosecond timestamp tracking, executable-quality pricing gates, and provider API mapping.

1.1 Executive Summary and System Objective

The Slayer Terminal project aims to architect and deploy an advanced, data-driven options research and signal-generation platform. The core objective is to identify, evaluate, rank, and publish high-quality options trade callouts to a web interface. The product transcends generic options scanners, which typically rely on rudimentary volume or open interest filters. Instead, Slayer Terminal synthesizes real-time market microstructure data, non-arbitrage volatility analytics, non-standard options activity, cross-asset macro indicators, and proprietary risk models. The system determines the optimal underlying asset, the precise options strategy, the exact expiration and strike structure, and the lifecycle management logic (entry, hold, adjustment, exit, or rejection).

Primary Architectural Constraint: Non-Executing Signal Engine

The platform operates strictly as an intelligence and signal-publishing engine; it does not connect to a brokerage or execute trades autonomously. This document serves as the foundational Product Requirements and Technical Design specification, establishing the data architecture, strategy taxonomy, mathematical models, validation plan, risk framework, and implementation roadmap required before authorizing production strategy code. The explicit goal is a differentiated, comprehensive options-intelligence platform that selects the most appropriate strategy for each opportunity and transparently documents both the mathematical edge and the structural risks.

1.2 Data Architecture, Timestamps, and Quality Controls

To manage the immense volume of options data—which routinely exceeds 100 billion messages per day across OPRA feeds and generates massive microbursts during volatile events—the system requires a robust, high-throughput time-series data architecture.

Storage and Normalization Architecture

The underlying storage relies on a time-series database optimized for high-frequency financial data, utilizing hypertables partitioned by timestamps to enable continuous aggregations and columnar compression. The architecture utilizes TimescaleDB to handle the ingestion of raw trades and quotes, ensuring that historical backtesting and real-time querying operate with minimal latency. Continuous aggregates allow the system to pre-calculate 1-minute and daily bars, dramatically reducing query times for historical volatility modeling.

Microstructure and Timestamp Precision

The platform requires nanosecond-precision timestamps to maintain the exact sequencing of market events and to prevent look-ahead bias during historical validation. The system records multiple timestamps for every event:

ts_event Matching engine timestamp recorded at exchange source.
ts_recv Capture server packet ingestion timestamp.
ts_in_delta Sending timestamp offset accounting for network latency.

By strictly querying against the ts_event timestamp bounded by the current system clock during shadow testing, the architecture guarantees point-in-time accuracy, completely isolating the simulation from future corporate actions, news ingestion, or closing open-interest figures.

Executable-Quality and Data-Health Gate

Before any signal generation occurs, a deterministic data-health gate evaluates the real-time tape. The system rejects opportunities where quotes are stale, markets are crossed, or required contract metadata is missing. For multi-leg positions, the system accounts for realistic package pricing. Theoretical midpoint pricing is strictly prohibited; the engine evaluates the natural price (buying the ask, selling the bid) across the National Best Bid and Offer (NBBO). If the resulting net debit or credit eliminates the statistical edge, or if liquidity stress suggests severe slippage, the setup transitions to a "No-Trade" state. All rejected candidates and their specific rejection rationales are recorded in a dedicated database ledger.

SECTION 2 — INTELLIGENCE ENGINES & MATHEMATICAL MODELS

Catalyst NLP, eSSVI Volatility Model & 20 Alpha Modules

Section Context & Purpose

2.1 Stock News and Catalyst Intelligence Engine

A proprietary Natural Language Processing (NLP) pipeline evaluates market catalysts. The engine continuously ingests headlines, automatically deduplicating repetitive syndications and scoring updates based on novelty, source reliability, and timestamp freshness. The engine utilizes entity linkage to map news not only to the directly mentioned ticker but also to affected suppliers, competitors, and sector ETFs.

Crucially, the news engine separates directional implications (e.g., an earnings beat signaling upside) from volatility implications (e.g., pending litigation signaling sustained IV expansion). The system measures the market's response immediately following a headline, analyzing price action, volume spikes, and IV skew adjustments. The "News Reaction Latency" module detects delayed repricing; a signal is generated only if a quantifiable, executable mispricing remains on the options chain after the initial market reaction has been absorbed by high-frequency market makers.

2.2 eSSVI Volatility Surface & SABR Model

The Implied Volatility and Surface-Relative Opportunity Detection module fits the empirical volatility surface to detect localized mispricings. The system utilizes the extended Surface Stochastic Volatility Inspired (eSSVI) parametrization, a model that generates a total-variance curve $w(k, \theta_t)$ in log-moneyness $k = \ln(K/S)$, guaranteeing a surface free of calendar spread and butterfly arbitrage:

$$w(k, \theta_t) = \frac{\theta_t}{2} \left[ 1 + \rho \phi k + \sqrt{(\phi k + \rho)^2 + (1 - \rho^2)} \right]$$

Where $\rho \in (-1, 1)$ governs skewness, $\phi > 0$ controls smooth curvature, and $\theta_t$ represents at-the-money variance. The model relies on a vega-weighted objective function minimized via the Levenberg-Marquardt optimizer. Complementing this, the Volatility Expansion and Contraction module applies the Stochastic Alpha Beta Rho (SABR) model to forecast future volatility states, calibrating beta ($\beta$) and nu ($\nu$, vol of vol) parameters to assess the probability of IV crush or expansion.

3.1 Expiration and Contract Coverage Rules

The strategy selection engine maps the opportunity's intended holding horizon to specific expirations, accounting for liquidity, Greek decay curves, and catalyst timing. Contract specifications, exercise styles (American vs. European), and settlement types (physical vs. cash) are strictly evaluated.

3.2 Full Strategy Catalog and Phased Rollout

The strategy taxonomy is divided into distinct families. The engine evaluates the complete payoff distribution rather than selecting the cheapest contract. Multi-leg strategies are modeled as complete mathematical positions.

PHASE 1: SINGLE-LEG STRATEGIES Fundamental Rollout
Long Calls and Long Puts:

Deployed for high-confidence directional forecasts where implied volatility is relatively inexpensive, and expected move exceeds breakeven. Rejection occurs if IV percentile is high or bid/ask spread exceeds slippage limits. Short-term momentum favors high-gamma short-DTE ATM options; macro theses favor lower-theta deep ITM (0.70+ delta) options. Risk Tier 2 or 3.

Covered Calls and Cash-Secured Puts (CSPs):

Deployed in neutral to mildly directional regimes to harvest volatility premium and theta decay. Typically 30–45 DTE, targeting 0.20 to 0.30 delta strikes to balance income with assignment risk. Max gain capped at premium received plus stock appreciation. Risk Tier 2.

Protective Puts & Naked Shorts Distinction:

Protective puts act as portfolio insurance (Tier 1). Naked short options possess undefined tail risk and are restricted to analytical tracking or severe Portfolio Margin overpricing setups (Tier 4).

PHASE 2: VERTICAL SPREADS Core Directional Engine
Bull Call / Bear Put Debit Spreads:

Deployed when IV is moderate to high to offset long option cost and theta decay through the short leg. Long strike placed ATM; short strike placed at target price. Max Loss is net debit; Max Gain is strike width minus debit. Risk Tier 1/2.

Bull Put / Bear Call Credit Spreads:

Deployed for directional or neutral theses where IV is elevated and expected to undergo volatility crush. Short strikes placed outside expected move (0.15–0.25 delta). Mandatory exit before market close on expiration day to prevent pin risk. Risk Tier 2.

PHASE 3, 4 & 5: VOLATILITY, CALENDARS & MULTI-LEG STRUCTURES Advanced Expansion
Long Straddles & Strangles (Phase 3): Deployed when catalyst modules predict movement exceeding market implied pricing (e.g., unpriced earnings shock). Requires raw multi-leg package quote validation on OPRA.
Calendar & Diagonal Spreads (Phase 4): Exploits term structure slope mispricings (contango vs backwardation) across differing expiration cycles.
Broken-Wing Butterflies (BWBs) & Iron Condors (Phase 5): Skips a strike on the upper wing to enter for a net credit, completely eliminating downside risk if the stock drops sharply.

SECTION 5 — SIGNAL PUBLISHING & LIFECYCLE LOGIC

5-State Lifecycle Machine & OpenAPI Schema

Section Context & Purpose

This section details the state transition logic for signals within Slayer Terminal, governing their lifecycle from detection to exit or rejection. It also provides the standardized OpenAPI 3.1 JSON schema payloads for signal publication and no-trade audit logs.

5.1 Deterministic 5-State Lifecycle Machine

Every identified opportunity transitions through a deterministic 5-state lifecycle machine. Active signals are continually monitored by a Counterfactual Hold Audit Loop to determine whether maintaining the position yields higher expected value ($EV$) than liquidating at current natural NBBO market prices.

STATE 1 WAIT

Thesis active; waiting for technical or IV entry trigger.

STATE 2 NO-TRADE

Gate rejection: stale quotes, crossed markets, or high slippage.

STATE 3 ENTRY

Passed health gate. Signal published with max cost limit.

STATE 4 HOLD

Counterfactual audit: remaining $EV$ exceeds liquidation cost.

STATE 5 EXIT

Target hit, thesis invalidated, time stop, or deteriorating liquidity.

SECTION 6 — SYSTEM VALIDATION & ROADMAP

Validation, Limitations & 15-Phase Roadmap

Section Context & Purpose

This concluding section presents the quantitative validation framework, including out-of-sample walk-forward testing and feed ablation modeling. It transparently outlines system limitations and establishes the complete 15-phase engineering implementation roadmap.

6.1 Walk-Forward & Feed Ablation Testing

Before production deployment, Slayer Terminal enforces walk-forward out-of-sample testing, feed ablation validation, and a mandatory 30-day live shadow period. Feed ablation measures the net out-of-sample Sharpe Ratio contribution of each data feed. Any endpoint failing to demonstrate incremental value after slippage drag is deprecated.

Out-of-Sample Sharpe Ratio Impact Upon Feed Ablation

Highest incremental alpha generators: OPRA Nanosecond NBBO (+0.82 Sharpe) and eSSVI Vol Surface (+0.64 Sharpe).

6.2 Documented System Limitations

1. Execution Price Drift

Because signals are published rather than auto-executed, latency drift occurs. Mitigated by max_acceptable_net_cost filters.

2. FLEX Options Exclusion

Custom institutional FLEX options are excluded due to lack of continuous OPRA public order book quotes.

3. Legging Execution Risk

Multi-leg positions assume fill as complete complex packages. Manual leg-in strategies introduce unmodeled execution risk.

4. House Margin Discrepancies

Individual brokerages enforce house margin buffers that may exceed standard Reg T requirements, restricting retail trade execution.
